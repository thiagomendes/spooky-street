# Spooky Street
## Product and implementation specification • v1.4 • First playable case

**Case title:** Three Knocks on Lantern Lane  
**Format:** single-player, third-person 3D neighborhood mystery with rich 3D-platformer movement, in landscape. English ships inside the app. Downloadable languages are a release-one capability.  
**First audience (v1.4):** curious children about 6–9, with optional detective's extras for players up to 12, in one hybrid pictures-and-words mode (D23.1, D27).  
**Delivery:** solo developer using Claude Code cloud sessions and GitHub Actions, Unity/C#/URP, iPhone via TestFlight first, Android kept playable.  
**v1.2 amendment (owner, 2026-11-21):** the owner's intent is a game whose movement, camera, UI and look feel like a modern, polished 3D platformer while keeping this mystery. Pip gains a full, joyful moveset and the block becomes a vertical playground with optional collectibles (§4, §5). Real 3D models replace primitives early (§6). Draw on genre conventions only: never reproduce a specific game's characters, signature mechanics, level layouts, UI layouts, music or art.
**v1.3 amendment (owner, 2026-11-21):** every asset is created from scratch for this game; nothing is downloaded. The product bar is an extremely premium game: UI/UX, graphics, character detail, environments, rich interactions and density are top priorities (§6 *Premium bar*).
**v1.4 amendment (owner, 2026-11-22):** mystery first (D26): the child gathers the clues and makes the deduction; witnesses, captions, hints, replays and cutscenes never state a conclusion before the child does. The narrative bible and its companions are the north star (D27, §11). Audience 6–9 with extras up to 12; a season designed for eight cases, four committed first, open-ended; short in-engine cutscenes serve the story (§6). Edits in §1–§9 are marked *(v1.4)*.
**Scope of this specification:** one complete case at intended release quality. The season is designed for eight cases with four committed first and stays open for later seasons (D27, `docs/world-and-cases.md` §4); each further case is approved from its own design doc. Commercial launch is a separate decision after playtesting. Figures marked as targets are starting hypotheses. Record actual device results before claiming them.

## 1. The promise

At the foot of a tree you notice a bright snag of cloth. From the sidewalk it looked like party tinsel. From the treehouse you can see a girl in the attic wearing a cape with a piece missing. You worked that out by moving through a place you now understand a little better. That is the central pleasure of Spooky Street.

The opening image is a child-sized view past a candy bowl toward a dark blue house. Its round attic window lights up. Three uneven knocks travel down the street. A small paper bat falls sideways off a porch. The ending returns to the same composition. The house is open, its owner is sharing candy, and this time the player knows exactly what will happen when the wind turns the rooftop owl.

**Priority order (v1.4):** a mystery the child solves (questions raised by the world, clues found by curiosity, the child's own deduction) → pleasurable movement and camera (the platformer moveset is the main way to explore) → memorable, dense block → polished mobile presentation → content volume. When time runs short, cut the optional discovery or shorten a conversation before compromising the core route. A new street requires a new product decision. Every stage plan and case passes the narrative bible's checklist (`docs/narrative-bible.md` §6) before it is built. The base experience includes local progress, offline completion and optional hints. Keep combat, lives, countdowns, currencies, inventories of consumables, accounts, ads and runtime AI outside this case. Collectibles (§4) are counted, never spent.

**What actually happened:** Kit’s yard has a weather-powered owl puppet. Wind turns a small rooftop wheel. A shaft drives a three-lobed wooden cam below the entry floor. Its three taps normally accompany the owl bowing at the ticket window. The linkage to the puppet slipped loose, leaving the knocks sounding from inside a shuttered house. June switched on the attic lantern on arrival; the knocks preceded her visit. For continuity, the opening shows the first light *Pip witnesses*, followed by a fresh cycle of knocks. At the finale Pip reseats the captive linkage with one interaction, then turns the test crank. The street sees the owl bow three times. The explanation is visible even with all text skipped. Final feeling: a place that looked frightening becomes somewhere the player helped bring back to life.

## 3. Case script and progression contract

Aim for 12–18 minutes on a first unassisted visit by an older reader, about 17–30 for a 6–8-year-old with assisted reading (v1.4, `docs/mystery-first.md` §4), 4–7 on replay. These are level-design targets, never timers.

**Solvability rules.** The world accepts correct actions early. Entering the attic first records the route and June acknowledges that shortcut. After opening the door, later witness lines use the past tense (“It was shut when I looked”). The show requires frontOpen and testedOwl plus arriving at the porch; (v1.4) the “Case closed” stamp also requires the three solved-question facts. Optional conversations, bench, cloth and extra clues enrich the notebook; none can deadlock the case. Missing clues remain discoverable after closure. At the workshop, the exposed arm and crank invite interaction in order; touching the crank early produces a tap and a visible gap where the arm should connect. Repeated interactions change no persistent state twice. A reset during a reveal reconstructs the world from saved facts. (v1.4) No sticker placement order deadlocks; every accepted set is reachable from clues on the mandatory route; the decoy never completes a question; solved facts are additive, and older saves derive them once from their old proofs.

**Notebook (v1.4, deduction).** One page per question: “Where are the knocks coming from?”, “How did June get in?”, “What should the knocks move?”. Every found clue is a sticker in one shared tray; clues still in the world show only as generic “?” outlines, never per question. The child places stickers on the question’s 2–3 slots by drag or tap-tap. A wrong set wobbles home gently (no buzzer, no count, no penalty, no timer), with a progressive scaffold from the third try; a right set draws the answer comic from the child’s own stickers. The page never shows which clue belongs where, nor an answer before the child’s placement. Mechanic and accepted sets: `docs/mystery-first.md` §3 and §5. The final card shows the player’s actual discoveries. Quietly offer “Back to the street” and a separate confirmed “Replay case” action.

**Routes:** main accessible loop: plaza → yard → ramp → bridge → attic → foyer → porch → plaza. Skilled ascent: yard → fixed crate → sloped awning → treehouse; rejoins before the evidence. Start ramp at 1:5, minimum usable width 1.6 m. Start optional gaps at 0.7–1.0 m. Mandatory route is traversable without precision jumping. **Density review:** walk the block at normal speed and log the time between meaningful decisions, changing vistas, evidence or character responses. Target at least one every 15–25 seconds on first approach. Every mandatory space must support two distinct uses from the table. Count the same repeated decoration once. Mark three screenshots from the sidewalk, treehouse and bench with what each reveals and hides. If a section fails, shorten or restage it. Add space only when it creates a new inference or useful route.

**Vertical playground and collectibles (v1.2):** every rooftop, awning, fence top, hedge top and the treehouse is reachable by some combination of the moveset, so skilled players can cross the block without touching the street. Hide 30 **paper bats** (folded black-paper collectibles that flutter when Pip is near) across the block: about a third on the main loop, a third on skilled routes and a third behind small movement challenges (glide gaps, chain-jump ledges, wall-jump chimneys). The notebook counts them and every 10 unlock a sketch page (v1.4: carrying lore and seeds for later cases) and a costume piece or cape colour for Pip (`docs/engagement.md` §1). Paper bats never gate the mystery or a clue, never respawn, and are never spent. Movement challenges stay short (under 30 s), restart from the nearest safe anchor and never require the moves listed as optional below on the mandatory route.

## 5. Movement, camera and interaction

- **Moveset (v1.2).** Everything uses the stick plus Jump and Use, so touch stays two buttons. *Mandatory route:* walk/run and a basic jump only. *Optional, for exploration and paper bats:* variable jump height (tap ≈ 1 m, hold ≈ 1.6 m); chain jump (jump again within 0.2 s of landing while running: 1 m → 1.6 m → 2.4 m with a flip on the third); **bat-cape glide** (hold Jump while falling: Pip spreads the cape like bat wings and fall speed drops to about 2 m/s, steerable; Pip's own signature move); wall slide and wall jump; automatic ledge grab with climb-up; roll on the ground and short forward dive in the air on Use when no interaction target is in range. Every move has its own animation, squash-and-stretch, dust and sound, and keeps coyote time and buffering.
- Start with responsive screen-relative analog walking/running, maximum speed 4 m/s (v1.2: running ramps to 6 m/s after 0.6 s of full stick), roughly 0.15 s to accelerate and 0.12 s to stop. Jump apex near 1 m; initial coyote time 0.10 s and jump buffer 0.12 s. Expose these values as inspector data. Ankle-level steps are automatic. Input and simulation use consistent time semantics across 30/60 fps.
- Landscape safe areas. Left thumb uses a floating stick; right has Jump and contextual Use, plus drag on empty space to orbit. Initial button hit areas at least 48 logical points equivalent; validate scaling on device. Left-handed layout mirrors clusters. Support keyboard/mouse and gamepad via identical action maps. Holding Use never repeatedly advances dialogue.
- Camera starts behind Pip at about 3.8 m distance, slightly above shoulder line, target near upper torso. Bias framing toward the landing surface during traversal. Delay auto-recenter until movement stabilizes; manual orbit wins immediately. Shorten camera boom against solid walls, ease it out after clearance. Entering the attic blends to a closer shoulder view. Doorframes and hedges must never conceal both Pip and the next landing. Offer invert-Y, sensitivity, reduced motion and recenter.
- Choose Use target within about 1.6 m and in sight, weighted toward camera center. Latch the selected target while pressed. Clear selection on menu, dialogue or lost focus. When June stands near a latch, separate their use markers spatially. Show icon + localized verb such as “Talk”, “Look”, “Sit” or “Turn”. A child should never need to aim at the thin crank handle.
- A fall uses the most recent safe ground anchor. Keep clues and puzzle progress. Landings have soft footfall, one flutter of the cape and restrained haptics (toggle). Idle cape and bat-ear motion settles after a moment so the player can watch clues. No fall damage.
- Pause suspends input, simulation and looping dialogue cues. Returning from background opens paused. Interfaces: title with Continue/New game, pause, notebook, settings, language manager, credits. First start goes quickly to the porch (v1.4: through the skippable opening cutscene, §6). Continue shows a picture recap: the last solved comic, the open question's chips and where Pip stands. New game with an existing save asks for confirmation. Hints on demand via radio (v1.4) help with where to look and how to get there, never with what it means: tier 1 nudges toward the *area* holding an unfound clue (a breeze, a witness’s glance, one sensory line such as “A breeze keeps tugging toward the side yard.”); tier 2 flies the bat trail to that area; tier 3 only demonstrates a move, when the blocker is a move. A stuck deduction gets Pip’s musing and the missing clue’s place, never the pairing (`docs/mystery-first.md` §6). Helper stays off by default (D23). Provide equivalent text for every spoken or environmental clue.

## 6. Art, animation and sound direction

**Art brief: a handmade neighborhood after sunset.** Build chunky silhouettes with bevels that catch porch light, painted timber, stitched costumes and paper decorations with slightly curled edges. Camera height makes the town feel built for children. The owl’s round eye repeats the attic window shape; silver fabric repeats June’s cape shape. Keep roofs, costumes, audio cues, typography and UI original. Create a provenance list for each imported asset, including license and attribution. Greybox primitives are valid through the route test; hero props must be recognizable at phone size before visual approval.

**Original art only (v1.3).** No downloaded, stock, kit or generated-by-third-party models, textures, animations, sounds or UI art. Every mesh, rig, animation, texture, shader, VFX and UI element is authored for Spooky Street, as versioned scripts where possible (Blender Python for models, rigs and animation; C# and shader code for materials, lighting, VFX and UI), so each asset is reviewable, reproducible and replaceable. Fonts stay licensed OFL faces (see provenance). Each asset ships a turnaround or in-context render reviewed against the art bible before it enters the game.

**Premium bar (v1.3).** The target is a handcrafted, toy-diorama look a player would expect from a top-tier 3D platformer, achieved with original work:
- *Form:* chunky, bevelled, subdivided shapes with clear silhouettes; no raw primitives or hard 90° edges in the final game. Hero characters have faces that act (eye blinks, brows, mouth shapes, look-at) and secondary motion (cape cloth, bat ears, hair and fringe on springs).
- *Surface and light:* hand-painted-style gradients and soft ambient occlusion, a custom toon-ramp lit shader with rim light tuned to the palette, warm practical lights with bloom, moonlight shadows; every material from the palette families.
- *Density and life:* a meaningful detail at least every ~1.5 m along walkable paths (props, trim, planters, decorations, wear); ambient life everywhere (leaves skittering, moths circling lamps, bats crossing the moon, candles flickering, curtains breathing, party sounds) that never competes with evidence.
- *Interactions:* every interactable has anticipation → action → reaction with animation, sound, particles and haptics; touching the world (bushes rustle, pumpkins wobble, lanterns swing) rewards curiosity even when it is not evidence.
- *UI/UX:* motion-designed screens (eased 150–300 ms transitions, staggered entrances, tactile press states), an illustrated title scene, a paper notebook that feels physical, sound on every control, clear hierarchy and zero placeholder labels.
- *Acceptance:* the visual scorecard bar is 4 of 5 on every axis for anything called finished (3 remains the floor for work in progress), judged on rendered captures and confirmed on the phone.

**Cutscenes (v1.4).** Short story moments, about 10–40 s in beats, made in-engine with the real models, cameras and world (no prerecorded video), non-verbal with the hybrid text line (D23.1), always skippable and rewatchable from the notebook. They open the game (Pip arriving as the new kid, ending on the arrival-porch composition so play still starts there in control), stage a case’s question, pay off a deduction after the child has solved it (staged from the child’s own clues), show what the street remembers, plant the next case’s seed and carry the season finale. They never reveal an answer before the child has deduced it and never show what the child could discover by playing. Rules: `docs/narrative-bible.md` §5.

## 8. Performance, accessibility and proof of quality

Target stable 60 fps on the owner’s iPhone. Record exact model, OS, renderer, build type, quality and ambient test conditions on first device pass. As initial acceptance, after 15 minutes of repeated exterior/interior traversal, target 95th-percentile frame time ≤18 ms, 99th ≤33.3 ms, and no recurring multi-frame freezes at the door. Report measured timings alongside the 16.7 ms ideal budget. On the chosen Android reference, target stable 30 fps or better; record tier and model. Compare memory after warm-up versus five complete loops to catch unbounded growth. Measure time to control on cold start (initial goal ≤8 s), resume, touch response and battery/thermal degradation. Measure in player builds. Prioritize lower shadow distance, fewer translucent layers and baked lighting before cutting evidence silhouettes. Keep debug overlays out of release builds.
