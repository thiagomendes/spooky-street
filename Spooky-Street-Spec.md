# Spooky Street
## Product and implementation specification • v1.2 • First playable case

**Case title:** Three Knocks on Lantern Lane  
**Format:** single-player, third-person 3D neighborhood mystery with rich 3D-platformer movement, in landscape. English ships inside the app. Downloadable languages are a release-one capability.  
**First audience:** curious children roughly 8–12, with assisted play for younger readers and optional deductions for older players.  
**Delivery:** solo developer using Claude Code cloud sessions and GitHub Actions, Unity/C#/URP, iPhone via TestFlight first, Android kept playable.  
**v1.2 amendment (owner, 2026-11-21):** the owner's intent is a game whose movement, camera, UI and look feel like a modern, polished 3D platformer while keeping this mystery. Pip gains a full, joyful moveset and the block becomes a vertical playground with optional collectibles (§4, §5). Real 3D models replace primitives early: CC0 base kits re-dressed to the palette plus original hero pieces (§6). Draw on genre conventions only: never reproduce a specific game's characters, signature mechanics, level layouts, UI layouts, music or art.
**Scope of this specification:** one complete case at intended release quality. Future cases and commercial launch are separate decisions after playtesting. Figures marked as targets are starting hypotheses. Record actual device results before claiming them.

## 1. The promise

At the foot of a tree you notice a bright snag of cloth. From the sidewalk it looked like party tinsel. From the treehouse you can see a girl in the attic wearing a cape with a piece missing. You worked that out by moving through a place you now understand a little better. That is the central pleasure of Spooky Street.

The opening image is a child-sized view past a candy bowl toward a dark blue house. Its round attic window lights up. Three uneven knocks travel down the street. A small paper bat falls sideways off a porch. The ending returns to the same composition. The house is open, its owner is sharing candy, and this time the player knows exactly what will happen when the wind turns the rooftop owl.

**Priority order:** readable mystery → pleasurable movement and camera (the platformer moveset is the main way to explore) → memorable, dense block → polished mobile presentation → content volume. When time runs short, cut the optional discovery or shorten a conversation before compromising the core route. A new street requires a new product decision. The base experience includes local progress, offline completion and optional hints. Keep combat, lives, countdowns, currencies, inventories of consumables, accounts, ads and runtime AI outside this case. Collectibles (§4) are counted, never spent.

## 2. Cast, story truth and emotional arc

| Character | Silhouette and manner | Knowledge and playable role |
|---|---|---|
| Pip, the player | Short rain cape over a homemade moth costume. Soft antennae bounce once on landing. A small torch clips to the satchel. | Curious and observant. The player chooses where to look. Pip sketches discoveries with icons, never voices a deduction before the player acts. |
| Bea, the cyclist | Pumpkin helmet, bicycle festooned with foil moons. Talks quickly; steadies the spinning front wheel when listening. | Recognized her older sister June’s silver cape beside the house. Her view of June’s feet was blocked by the hedge. |
| Mr. Vale, the neighbor | Seated beside a candy bowl, dressed as a cardboard knight. His visor slips when he leans forward. | Was watching the front door from the bench. Correctly remembers that it stayed shut. Invites Pip to sit and check. |
| June, the missing witness | Silver comet cape with a triangular tear. Repeats the knock rhythm by tapping her knee. | Entered through the old treehouse entrance when the owl lantern came on. Came downstairs to find the sound, tried the sticky front door, then went back up to signal from the attic. |
| Aunt Kit, the owner | Seen in a framed workshop photo and at the final porch reveal; painted owl pin on her coat. | Gave the children permission to use the treehouse and help prepare her old Halloween puppet booth. Returns with the treats for the reopening. Her old installation explains the mystery. |

**What actually happened:** Kit’s yard has a weather-powered owl puppet. Wind turns a small rooftop wheel. A shaft drives a three-lobed wooden cam below the entry floor. Its three taps normally accompany the owl bowing at the ticket window. The linkage to the puppet slipped loose, leaving the knocks sounding from inside a shuttered house. June switched on the attic lantern on arrival; the knocks preceded her visit. For continuity, the opening shows the first light *Pip witnesses*, followed by a fresh cycle of knocks. At the finale Pip reseats the captive linkage with one interaction, then turns the test crank. The street sees the owl bow three times. The explanation is visible even with all text skipped. Final feeling: a place that looked frightening becomes somewhere the player helped bring back to life.

## 3. Case script and progression contract

Aim for 12–18 minutes on a first unassisted visit, 4–7 on replay. These are level-design targets, never timers.

**Solvability rules.** The world accepts correct actions early. Entering the attic first awards the route proof and June acknowledges that shortcut. After opening the door, later witness lines use the past tense (“It was shut when I looked”). The ending requires frontOpen and testedOwl plus arriving at the porch. Optional conversations, bench, cloth and extra clues enrich the notebook; none can deadlock the case. Missing clues remain discoverable after closure. At the workshop, the exposed arm and crank invite interaction in order; touching the crank early produces a tap and a visible gap where the arm should connect. Repeated interactions change no persistent state twice. A reset during a reveal reconstructs the world from saved facts.

**Notebook.** One illustrated spread with three questions: “How did June get inside?”, “Where are the knocks coming from?”, “What did the knocking move?” Fill each answer after physical proof. Unknown entries use a question mark and a silhouette. Optional evidence appears as small annotations, with a short caption on tap. The final card shows the player’s actual discoveries. Quietly offer “Back to the street” and a separate confirmed “Replay case” action.

## 4. Lantern Lane: a compact, buildable map

Block out approximately 44 × 36 m. Unity units are meters, north is +Z. Origin at the plaza. Dimensions guide the first build; change them when the phone camera or playtest calls for it. From the start, show the round attic window above the hedge. Reserve clear negative space around it. Decorative roofs at the boundary supply skyline only. Visually close inaccessible alleys with occupied party tables, fences or hedges.

|---|---|---|

| Plaza (0,0; ground) | Three choices readable together: bicycle to west, bench to east, blue porch north. A maple breaks the space asymmetrically. | Return hub for hints and observation; three visible ribbons twitch when the owl turns. |

| Shed and treehouse (−8,+13; 1.2/2.4 m) | Broad ramp and platforms with railings. Short skilled route via crate and awning. | Cloth clue; look back to see exactly why Bea and Vale saw different things. |

| Workshop nook (+4,+13; ground) | Low grille, blunt wooden mechanism, striped linkage and hand crank at Pip’s height. | Turning the crank repeats the puppet show after closure; a friend outside bows along. |

**Routes:** main accessible loop: plaza → yard → ramp → bridge → attic → foyer → porch → plaza. Skilled ascent: yard → fixed crate → sloped awning → treehouse; rejoins before the evidence. Start ramp at 1:5, minimum usable width 1.6 m. Start optional gaps at 0.7–1.0 m. Mandatory route is traversable without precision jumping. Keep the shed solid and ledges thick enough to read in motion. Falling returns Pip to a known safe surface within two seconds.

**Density review:** walk the block at normal speed and log the time between meaningful decisions, changing vistas, evidence or character responses. Target at least one every 15–25 seconds on first approach. Every mandatory space must support two distinct uses from the table. Count the same repeated decoration once. Mark three screenshots from the sidewalk, treehouse and bench with what each reveals and hides. If a section fails, shorten or restage it. Add space only when it creates a new inference or useful route.

**Vertical playground and collectibles (v1.2):** every rooftop, awning, fence top, hedge top and the treehouse is reachable by some combination of the moveset, so skilled players can cross the block without touching the street. Hide 30 **paper moths** (folded warm-paper collectibles that flutter when Pip is near) across the block: about a third on the main loop, a third on skilled routes and a third behind small movement challenges (glide gaps, chain-jump ledges, wall-jump chimneys). The notebook counts them and every 10 unlock a page of Pip's sketches or a cape colour. Paper moths never gate the mystery, never respawn, and are never spent. Movement challenges stay short (under 30 s), restart from the nearest safe anchor and never require the moves listed as optional below on the mandatory route.

## 5. Movement, camera and interaction

- **Moveset (v1.2).** Everything uses the stick plus Jump and Use, so touch stays two buttons. *Mandatory route:* walk/run and a basic jump only. *Optional, for exploration and paper moths:* variable jump height (tap ≈ 1 m, hold ≈ 1.6 m); chain jump (jump again within 0.2 s of landing while running: 1 m → 1.6 m → 2.4 m with a flip on the third); **moth glide** (hold Jump while falling: the costume wings open and fall speed drops to about 2 m/s, steerable; Pip's own signature move); wall slide and wall jump; automatic ledge grab with climb-up; roll on the ground and short forward dive in the air on Use when no interaction target is in range. Every move has its own animation, squash-and-stretch, dust and sound, and keeps coyote time and buffering.
- Start with responsive screen-relative analog walking/running, maximum speed 4 m/s (v1.2: running ramps to 6 m/s after 0.6 s of full stick), roughly 0.15 s to accelerate and 0.12 s to stop. Jump apex near 1 m; initial coyote time 0.10 s and jump buffer 0.12 s. Expose these values as inspector data. Ankle-level steps are automatic. Input and simulation use consistent time semantics across 30/60 fps.
- Landscape safe areas. Left thumb uses a floating stick; right has Jump and contextual Use, plus drag on empty space to orbit. Initial button hit areas at least 48 logical points equivalent; validate scaling on device. Left-handed layout mirrors clusters. Support keyboard/mouse and gamepad via identical action maps. Holding Use never repeatedly advances dialogue.
- Camera starts behind Pip at about 3.8 m distance, slightly above shoulder line, target near upper torso. Bias framing toward the landing surface during traversal. Delay auto-recenter until movement stabilizes; manual orbit wins immediately. Shorten camera boom against solid walls, ease it out after clearance. Entering the attic blends to a closer shoulder view. Doorframes and hedges must never conceal both Pip and the next landing. Offer invert-Y, sensitivity, reduced motion and recenter.
- Choose Use target within about 1.6 m and in sight, weighted toward camera center. Latch the selected target while pressed. Clear selection on menu, dialogue or lost focus. When June stands near a latch, separate their use markers spatially. Show icon + localized verb such as “Talk”, “Look”, “Sit” or “Turn”. A child should never need to aim at the thin crank handle.
- A fall uses the most recent safe ground anchor. Keep clues and puzzle progress. Landings have soft footfall, one squash of the satchel and restrained haptics (toggle). Idle antenna motion settles after a moment so the player can watch clues. No fall damage.
- Pause suspends input, simulation and looping dialogue cues. Returning from background opens paused. Interfaces: title with Continue/New game, pause, notebook, settings, language manager, credits. First start goes quickly to the porch. New game with an existing save asks for confirmation. Hints on demand via radio: first observation, then precise location, then direct action. Example stage two: “Try Mr. Vale’s seat. What does the hedge hide?” Provide equivalent text for every spoken or environmental clue.

## 6. Art, animation and sound direction

**Art brief: a handmade neighborhood after sunset.** Build chunky silhouettes with bevels that catch porch light, painted timber, stitched costumes and paper decorations with slightly curled edges. Camera height makes the town feel built for children. The owl’s round eye repeats the attic window shape; silver fabric repeats June’s cape shape. Keep roofs, costumes, audio cues, typography and UI original. Create a provenance list for each imported asset, including license and attribution. Greybox primitives are valid through the route test; hero props must be recognizable at phone size before visual approval.

**Model sourcing (v1.2).** Use real 3D models from S000.2 onward. Base kits: CC0 packs in one coherent chunky low-poly style (KayKit by Kay Lousberg: Halloween Bits, Character Pack Adventures and compatible packs), re-dressed to this palette and recoloured so no prop reads as a stock asset at hero distance. Characters start from a CC0 rigged base with its animation set (idle, walk, run, jump start/air/land, dodge/roll, interact, pick up, sit, cheer) and get original silhouettes: Pip's moth antennae, wings and amber rain cape; Bea's pumpkin helmet; June's silver comet cape; Mr. Vale and Kit as written. Original hero pieces (owl house and booth, cam linkage, rooftop owl and wheel, June's cape) are modelled in Blender from versioned scripts so they stay reviewable and replaceable. Every imported file is listed in `docs/asset-provenance.md` with source, licence and modification.

## 7. Persistence, localization and platform architecture

Use one Unity 6.3 LTS patch verified against the available toolchain; commit ProjectVersion.txt, manifest, package lock, settings and .meta files. C#, URP, Input System, Cinemachine, Localization and Addressables. Keep platform services at the boundary. Git LFS for large source binaries if needed. One gameplay scene is adequate for this block; keep UI and saved data independent of scene object names. Prefer a small explicit fact reducer and authored interaction components to a generic quest engine. Use fixed IDs such as c01.attic.entered and c01.workshop.linked. Map the beat-table names to stable IDs in one definition. No online dependency for gameplay.

**Save contract:** schema version, case ID, acquired facts, last safe anchor ID, discovered notebook entries. Store preferences and language independently. Autosave on fact changes, closure and safe transition; serialize writes, write temporary file then atomically replace and preserve one previous valid copy. On resume, restore props from facts before enabling controls. Unknown anchor → plaza. Newer unsupported schema → explain and preserve its file. Corrupt latest → recover previous or offer explicit fresh start. Replaying resets only case data. Exercise interruption after window entry, latch opening and immediately after crank interaction. Retry never duplicates rewards, resets doors or strands June.

**Language packs are a v1 feature.** Bundle English strings, fonts and essential localized assets. Externalize prompts, notebook, choices, captions, settings, credits, errors, accessibility text and pluralized content. Use stable semantic keys and translator context. Keep text out of clue textures unless a localized asset substitute exists. Implement a language screen listing installed and compatible available packs with native name, size, progress and actions. Manifest fields: locale, content version, supported game-content schema, download size, bundle reference and integrity hash. Trust a configured HTTPS catalog host. Build bundles per target as required. Download to staging, validate version, integrity, font coverage and loadability, then atomically activate; retain old pack on failure. Missing individual key → bundled English. Offline → installed packs remain selectable. Removing active pack reverts to English. Never couple save progression to display strings. A static fixture host and a downloadable pseudo-language with +35% string expansion are sufficient for engineering acceptance. Hide test locale from production catalog. Test interruption, insufficient storage, old catalog, corrupt download, restart in airplane mode and case progress before/after language switch.

### Remote development and build workflow

The owner’s local notebook is an Alienware with an Intel Core i7, 16 GB RAM and a GPU described by the owner as having six cores. The exact GPU model is unspecified. Keep the notebook available for other projects. A phone or browser is enough to direct development. The first playable loop must succeed with the notebook switched off.

The cloud session can run its available checks and read CI results. Use the licensed CI editor for checks that need Unity.
2. **Verify the branch.** GitHub Actions runs applicable checks against the proposed commit. Record the exact Unity patch and test results. Keep signing credentials out of PR jobs. After review, select the approved commit for a device build.
3. **Export on a temporary GitHub-hosted worker.** Provide a manual “Build for TestFlight” action in `.github/workflows/ios-testflight.yml`. Check out the selected commit and LFS content, prepare a pinned Unity editor with iOS build support and an activation method compatible with the owner’s license, then invoke a versioned batch build method. Package the generated Xcode project, metadata and any required executable bits/symlinks as an artifact tagged with commit and workflow run. A clean runner must reproduce it without files from the Alienware. Surface activation or module failures before starting the macOS job.
4. **Archive and upload on a temporary GitHub-hosted macOS runner.** Retrieve the matching artifact, verify its provenance, select a compatible Xcode, sign using protected Apple credentials and upload to App Store Connect. Give retries a unique build number and report commit, build number and processing status. Keep secrets out of logs and artifacts; constrain signing to trusted revisions. Configure spending controls consistent with the owner's budget.
5. **Play on the iPhone.** Once Apple finishes processing, the owner installs through TestFlight and reports observations with the build number. Feed those observations into the next spec or PR. S000 passes end-to-end once a remotely produced build launches on the physical iPhone. Track missing credentials, service access or device feedback separately from verified checks.

**Optional visual editing:** use an on-demand GPU workstation only when interactive Unity Editor work is useful (camera composition, lighting, animation or scene dressing). Version the edits in Git. The normal CI and TestFlight workflow runs independently of that workstation.

Android: produce a development APK and exercise touch, lifecycle and save on a real device by the route milestone. Log unavailable credentials/hardware as pending, continue local work, and never mark an upload or physical test as passed without evidence.

## 8. Performance, accessibility and proof of quality

Target stable 60 fps on the owner’s iPhone. Record exact model, OS, renderer, build type, quality and ambient test conditions on first device pass. As initial acceptance, after 15 minutes of repeated exterior/interior traversal, target 95th-percentile frame time ≤18 ms, 99th ≤33.3 ms, and no recurring multi-frame freezes at the door. Report measured timings alongside the 16.7 ms ideal budget. On the chosen Android reference, target stable 30 fps or better; record tier and model. Compare memory after warm-up versus five complete loops to catch unbounded growth. Measure time to control on cold start (initial goal ≤8 s), resume, touch response and battery/thermal degradation. Measure in player builds. Prioritize lower shadow distance, fewer translucent layers and baked lighting before cutting evidence silhouettes. Keep debug overlays out of release builds.
