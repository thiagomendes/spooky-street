# S003.1: cartoon babble voices and generated music (D20.1)

Branch `feature/s003-voices-music`. This follows the owner's first listening review (D20.1): the effects were approved,
the formant voices were rejected as "strange", and the plucked opening motif was judged "mid". This increment makes
new voices and music from Google's generative models, post-processes them in `tools/audio/`, and publishes an owner
page, "Vozes e música v2". **Nothing is imported into `Assets/` yet.** The owner approves by ear first.

## Owner page

`docs/evidence/s003/listening_v2/index.html` (11 MB: MP3 previews, spectrograms and `report.json`). It has one card
per character, with phrases of three lengths, the five emotions and two examples sequenced at runtime with their
captions. It has one card per music cue, with three options, loop-seam previews and a "what to listen for" line.
Rebuild it with `python3 tools/audio/build_v2.py`. This needs no network: it reads the committed sources.

## Pipeline

| Step | Script | Network |
|---|---|---|
| Babble sources (Gemini TTS) | `tools/audio/babble_sources.py` → `tools/audio/generated/voice/<char>/<emotion>_t<n>.flac` | yes, missing sources only |
| Music sources (Lyria) | `tools/audio/music_sources.py` → `tools/audio/generated/music/<cue>/s<seed>.mp3` | yes, missing sources only |
| Babble processing, bank, phrases | `tools/audio/babble_build.py` (`spookyaudio/babble.py`, `babble_spec.py`) | no |
| Music editing | `tools/audio/music_build.py` (`spookyaudio/music.py`, `music_spec.py`) | no |
| Owner page | `tools/audio/build_v2.py` (`spookyaudio/page_v2.py`) | no |
| Machine listening proxies | `tools/audio/review_sources.py`, `review_babble.py` → `generated/review/*.json` | yes |

- `spookyaudio/genai.py` is a minimal HTTPS client. It never holds a key: the session proxy injects it. Every request
  is logged in `generated/calls.jsonl` (time, model, purpose and status; no headers). `generated/manifest.json`
  records each source's model, prompt, voice, seed, generation config, response id and the model's structure text,
  plus the call totals.
- WAV masters go to `tools/audio/out/` (ignored), as in S003. The sources are kept in plain git
  (`.gitattributes`: `tools/audio/generated/**` is binary, not LFS). They total 38 MB: 8 MB of FLAC voice and
  30 MB of MP3 music. TTS and Lyria accept a seed but do not promise identical output, so the sources are what
  makes a rebuild reproducible. Three rejected music sources stay out of git and are marked `committed: false` in
  the manifest.

## Voices

**Model:** `gemini-3.8-flash-tts`. A probe of `gemini-2.5-pro-preview-tts` also worked, but the newer flash model
was natural enough and does not read directions aloud when the prompt says "Speaker direction (do not read
aloud): … Transcript: …".

**Cast.** The cast was chosen by audition: 16 voices, judged by an audio-understanding model for age, fit and
distinctness.

| Character | Prebuilt voice | Direction | Target pitch | Speed | Formants |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pip | Laomedeia | curious little girl, new in town | 400 Hz | 1.85× | half the pitch rise |
| Bea | Zephyr | fast, confident little sister | 450 Hz | 2.15× | 0.55 of the rise |
| Mr. Vale | Sadaltager | slow, kind, dry grandfather | 125 Hz | 1.4× | kept |
| June | Achernar | whispering secrets, holding a giggle | 370 Hz | 1.9× | 0.45 of the rise |
| Aunt Kit | Sulafat | theatrical, warm, ex-actress | 245 Hz | 1.65× | 0.3 of the rise |

**Sources.** There are 50 sources: 5 characters × 5 emotions (neutral, happy, surprised, thinking, whisper) × 2
takes. Each is a seeded made-up syllable string built from a per-character consonant and vowel palette. Words that
form English or Portuguese words are blocked (`AVOID`). A listening pass flagged "dizzy", "cookie" and "no", and
those three takes were regenerated with new strings. "Whoa" was left in as a universal interjection.

**Processing (`babble.py`).**

1. Each take is shifted so its median pitch lands on the character's target × an emotion factor (happy 1.1,
   surprised 1.2, thinking and whisper 0.95). The TTS voices change register a lot between emotions: Pip's
   neutral source sits at 191 Hz and her happy source at 412 Hz.
2. The formant part of the shift is a plain resample, which leaves no artefacts. TD-PSOLA then adds the rest of
   the pitch rise with the formants kept, and the speed-up. The pitch marks come from waveform-similarity
   tracking of each glottal cycle.
3. Each take is sliced into syllables at the envelope minima between energy peaks. Runs between pauses are kept
   whole.

The listening proxy ranked the transforms, from cleanest to worst:

| Transform | Artefact score (0 = clean) |
|---|---|
| Unprocessed source | 0–3 |
| Resample + PSOLA (shipped) | 2.5 |
| PSOLA, all formants kept | 5 |
| The first STFT formant warp + WSOLA | 6–8 |

**Outputs (per character).**

- Phrase takes: 6 per emotion, 30 in all, at 0.6–3.0 s. Each joins natural syllable runs, so the co-articulation
  is real.
- A syllable bank: up to 16 syllables per emotion, level-matched within 4 dB, in one sheet WAV each.
  `generated/babble_bank.json` holds the offsets, lengths, pitch and level of each syllable.
- Two sequenced examples built from real case 01 captions.

All 150 phrase takes and 10 examples pass the checks: −19.0 LUFS ±0.1 (voice category), true peak ≤ −1.05 dBTP,
no clipping or clicks.

**Runtime approach (proposed, not built).** The game picks a babble per line:

1. Count the caption's syllables. The babble length is 0.6 × syllables ÷ the character's syllable rate, clamped to
   0.6–3 s (`babble.plan_sequence`). Babble is a flavour; the text line carries the words.
2. Play the phrase take of that emotion closest to that length. This is the main path and the most natural one.
3. For variety or long lines, sequence from the bank, using the same rules as `plan_sequence`:
   - a seeded shuffle with no immediate repeat;
   - 90 ms and 160 ms pauses where the caption has a comma or a full stop;
   - a falling melody per breath group (picks nearest the contour, nudged ≤ 1.5 st with `AudioSource.pitch`);
   - a question ends on the highest syllable +2 st, an exclamation on the loudest;
   - equal-power 28 ms joins.

   The bank sheets slice into clips at load (`AudioClip.Create` from `GetData`).

The Python planner is unit-tested as the reference. A Core port (`Core/Audio/BabblePlanner` plus the JSON) is
simple and pure, but it was left for the import step so this branch has no Unity changes.

## Music

**Models.** `lyria-3-pro-preview` (served as `lyria-3.5`) for the loops, and `lyria-3-clip-preview` (about 30 s)
for the sting and the reveal.

What the probes found:

- Lyria takes `generationConfig.seed`.
- It ignores requested durations and timed sections: the pro model writes about 3-minute pieces, the clip model
  about 30 s.
- It does not accept audio input (it rejected an MP3 as `audio/s16le`), so the motif is carried by the text
  prompt.
- It returns MP3 at 44.1 kHz stereo, plus a structure text (`[[A0]] [[B1]] …`).

**Prompts.** Every prompt shares one motif sentence: the knock (short, short, long) rising D–F–A, answered by a
falling phrase. It also shares one instrument palette and one guard sentence: cosy, never scary, no vocals, choir,
organ or horror stingers. Prompts and seeds are in `music_spec.py` and the manifest.

**Editing (`music.py`).**

- **Loops.** A DP beat tracker and a meter test (3 vs 4 beats) feed a bar-to-bar loop search over 55–95 s. The
  score is the chroma and band similarity of 8 beats after both points and 4 beats before them, with a bonus for
  8-bar phrases. The seam is then waveform-aligned and given a 60 ms equal-power crossfade into the audio that led
  into the loop start.
- **Stings and reveals.** These are cut from the clip's natural ending. A sting starts on the strongest beat
  attack 5–8 s before the end; a reveal starts on the beat after the quietest second, 10–20 s before the end. The
  key of the ending is estimated (Krumhansl), and the cue is vari-sped by up to ±3 st so it ends on D or A, the
  score's tonal home. Lyria did not keep to D minor.

| Cue | Options on the page (seed) | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Street theme | 101, 303, 202 | 75.8 / 57.6 / 76.8 s loops | 101: 3/4 storybook waltz, D major, seam similarity 0.957. 202: has an accordion that was not asked for |
| Exploration | 111, 333, 222 | 76.8 / 64.0 / 76.8 s loops | 111 is the sparse twin of theme 101 (seam 0.99) |
| Case closed | 121, 242, 484 | 5.5–5.9 s | 121 and 242 were transposed −1 and +2 st to end in D |
| Reveal | 131, 524, 262 | 19.4 / 16.5 / 11.9 s | 131 starts hushed and blooms |

- **Rejected:** theme 404 (sunny guitar, not Halloween), reveal 393 (dark, with a low thump) and sting 363 (dry
  comic ending).
- **Mastering:** all cues reach −20.0 LUFS (music category) with true peak ≤ −3.1 dBTP. No limiting was needed.
- **Seams:** the 10 s seam previews (end into start) were judged seamless (0/10) by the listening proxy, and every
  loop passes the seam check.

## Listening proxies

Claude cannot listen, so it used three proxies:

- **The S003 checks:** loudness, true peak, clipping, DC, sub-bass, edge clicks and the loop seam.
- **Spectrograms**, reviewed by eye.
- **An audio-understanding model** (`gemini-3.8-flash`), used as a strict second opinion. Its answers are in
  `generated/review/`.

How reliable is that model? It separated the unprocessed sources (0–3) from the processed takes cleanly, but its
scores for the same clip vary by about ±1 between runs. Its timestamps and keys are unreliable, so it was used to
rank options, never to pass them.

Its final view of the babble:

- **Charm:** 5–7 out of 10.
- **Chipmunk:** 6–7 for the children and 3–4 for Vale.
- **Clean:** most phrase takes are rated clean or slightly "metallic".
- **Choppier:** the sequenced examples are rated choppier than the phrase takes, which is why phrase takes are
  the main runtime path.
- **Suggestions:** it keeps suggesting a slightly lower pitch for the children and for Kit.
