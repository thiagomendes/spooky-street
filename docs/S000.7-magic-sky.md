# S000.7: the handcrafted sky

Branch `feature/magic-sky`. Owner: "the sky is too basic"; the owner chose the **magical handcrafted sky**, in the same
paper-and-felt craft as the rest of the game (art bible §3, decision D22). Everything is original and procedural: no
downloaded textures, no new packages.

## What is in the sky

| Element | How it works | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Sky paint | A 1024 × 512 texture painted at load: horizon mist rising to a deep violet zenith, wobbling like brush strokes, on grained paper, with three faint paper-cut cloud strips near the horizon. It tiles around the dome. | `Core/Art/SkyPainter.cs` |
| Twinkling stars | 300 stars in a mask texture (R = brightness, G = twinkle phase). The dome shader pulses each star on its own phase and rate from a clock the game drives. The few big ones are four-point paper stars. | `SkyPainter.PaintStarMask`, `Art/Shaders/SkyDome.shader` |
| Moon | An 8.4 m paper disc (was 4.2 m) with cut-paper seas and eight felt craters, three with a stitched outline. It sits upper left of the arrival view, over the west neighbour's roof, clear of the maple that hid the old one. | `SkyPainter.PaintMoon`, `Runtime/World/MagicSky.cs` |
| Halo | A ring card behind the moon fading through vertex alpha (alpha-blended, 42 % at the disc), plus a broad per-vertex glow in the dome shader. | `SkyMeshes.Halo` |
| Paper clouds | Nine clouds on three rings that turn slowly about the dome's axis (0.32, 0.8 and 1.25 °/s), so they wrap with no pop. Each cloud is two opaque cut sheets with a deckled edge. Edges facing the moon, and every edge of a cloud passing in front of it, pick up a pale rim. The middle ring's first cloud is placed on the line from the arrival porch to the moon and starts across it within the first 10 s of play and covers it at about 30 s. | `Core/World/SkyShapes.cs`, `SkyMeshes.CloudRing`, `Art/Shaders/SkyPaper.shader` |
| Shooting star | One pooled streak (tapering trail and a four-point sparkle) every 20–40 s for 0.9 s, on a falling arc 12–52° up, within 50° of where the camera looks. | `Core/World/SkySchedule.cs`, `SkyModel.cs` |
| Bats | Two or three paper bat silhouettes cross in front of the moon every 26–52 s (the first after 9–18 s), flapping as a width and roll wobble. | `SkyShapes.Bat`, `SkyModel.BatPose` |

## Behaviour

- **One driver.** `MagicSky` is a sibling of `AmbientMotion`. It runs on scaled game time, so the title and pause
  screens (`timeScale` 0) hold the whole sky still, including a bat or streak caught mid-flight.
- **Reduced motion** (`AmbientMotion.ReducedMotion`): no shooting stars and no bats (running ones are dropped at
  once and the timers hold); clouds drift at a quarter speed; the twinkle slows to 30 %.
- **Value order.** The sky shaders clamp their output at 0.95, the moon paint peaks at 0.92 and the halo and streak
  are alpha-blended, never additive. Nothing in the sky reaches the bloom threshold (1), so the attic window stays the
  brightest point. No fog is applied to the sky.

## Cost

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Draw calls, steady | 5 (dome, moon disc, three crater discs) | 6 (dome, moon, halo, three cloud rings) |
| Draw calls, peak | 5 | 10 (plus three bats for 7.5 s and the streak for 0.9 s) |
| Triangles | about 1.5k dome + four cylinders | about 1.5k dome + 6k clouds + 0.6k moon, halo, bats and streak |
| Textures | 512 × 256 RGB | sky 1024 × 512 RGB, stars 1024 × 512 RG, moon 256², paper grain 128²: about 3 MB, no mip chains |
| Transparency | none | the halo card and the streak only; clouds, bats and moon are opaque cut meshes |

- Both shaders have one pass and no keywords, with every material property in `UnityPerMaterial` (written to be SRP
  Batcher compatible; no variants to strip).
- Painting the textures takes about 0.5 s of CPU on the cloud machine, once, at load. Worth timing on the phone.

## Fallbacks

- The shaders ship through two materials in `Content/Resources/Materials` (`Sky_Dome.mat`, `Sky_Paper.mat`).
- If a sky shader is missing, unsupported or failed to compile, `MagicSky` logs a warning and uses the committed URP
  Unlit base instead: the same sky paint with the stars baked in, the textured moon, flat-coloured clouds and bats.
  The halo and the shooting star need blending and are left out. Nothing draws magenta.

## Tests

- **Core (dotnet, local):** 241/241. `SkyTests` (14) covers the 20–40 s shooting-star interval over an hour of
  simulated play, the cloud wrap, the reduced-motion gating, the pause freeze, the bat flight over the disc, the paint
  tiling and gradient, the star mask and the cut shapes.
- **EditMode (`SkyDomeTests`):** moon, halo and cloud rings sit inside the dome and beyond the neighbour houses; pieces
  face the viewer; the sky shaders are referenced from `Resources` and have one pass and no keywords.
- **PlayMode (`MagicSkyTests`):** every sky renderer has a mesh and a sky or URP shader; the draw budget; the sky
  holds still on the title and pause screens and drifts in play; reduced motion removes the streak and the bats.
- **Visual:** new frames `16_sky_moon` and `17_shooting_star`. With a graphics device the capture test also requires
  the sky to be on its own shaders, not the fallback.
