# Spooky Street narrative bible (north star, approved 2026-11-22, D27)

Every stage plan, case design, vignette, line, hint, cutscene and mechanic is checked against this page. It is
grounded in `Spooky-Street-Spec.md` (v1.4, which points here), the art bible (`docs/art-bible.md` v2) and decisions
D14–D27. The owner approved it on 2026-11-22 (D27); §8 records the answers. Companion documents:

- `docs/mystery-first.md`: these rules applied to case 01 (audit, deduction mechanic, hints, proposed D26).
- `docs/world-and-cases.md`: the neighbourhood, the cast framework, the case template and the season arc.
- `docs/engagement.md`: why children come back, the hard guardrails for a paid-once Kids-category game, measures.
- `docs/roadmap.md`: where all of this lands in the stages, what to freeze, the spec amendments.

## 1. Purpose

Spec §1: "You worked that out by moving through a place you now understand a little better. That is the central
pleasure of Spooky Street." The game exists so a child feels **clever and brave in a friendly place**: a street
that looked a little frightening at dusk turns out to be full of kind people and clever old things, and the child
is the one who figured it out.

**Audience (decided 2026-11-22, D27).** The core is designed for children about **6–9**; optional **detective's
extras** (a bonus clue, an extra question or a harder link, never needed for the case stamp) give depth up to 12.
One hybrid mode of pictures and words (D23.1) serves pre-readers and readers alike.

## 2. Pillars

1. **The child is the detective.** The child gathers clues and makes the deduction. The game asks, the world
   answers when touched, and nobody solves it for them (owner, 2026-11-22). Spec: Pip "never voices a deduction
   before the player acts".
2. **Curiosity is always rewarded.** Every look, climb and touch gives something back: a reaction, a sound, a paper
   bat, a sticker, a joke. Spec §6 *Interactions*: "touching the world rewards curiosity even when it is not
   evidence"; spec §4 density review.
3. **Spooky, never scary; kind at the core.** Halloween night, knocks, shadows, but every mystery has a warm, human
   explanation and no villain. Spec §6: no abrupt scare, no chase, no blind dark passage. Art bible: a real street
   dressed for the night, humans in costumes, never a haunted ruin.
4. **Moving is exploring.** The platformer moveset (spec v1.2) is how the child reaches clues and sees the street
   from new angles; the treehouse view is how Bea and Vale's stories reconcile (CASE-03).
5. **A living neighbourhood that remembers.** What Pip does stays done: the booth reopens, Vale saves a sweet,
   friends greet Pip differently, across cases (decided 2026-11-22, D27; `world-and-cases.md` §5).

## 3. The street's through-line (decided 2026-11-22, D27)

- **Pip.** Pip moved to Lantern Lane this autumn and does not know anyone yet. She notices things
  because everything is new to her, and every case makes one more friend. Her bat cape and sketchbook notebook are
  her tools; she sketches, never lectures. The arc is belonging: from "the new kid" to "our detective".
- **The big mystery.** Decades ago Aunt Kit and her friends were **the Lantern Lane Players**: they
  built wind- and water-powered puppet machines all over the neighbourhood for a yearly **Night of Lanterns**. The
  troupe drifted apart and the machines fell quiet. Case 01's knocking owl is the first one Pip wakes. Each case
  wakes another machine and brings back someone tied to the Players. The finale relights the festival: the
  whole street performs together, and Pip is in the photo this time.
- **An open-ended season.** The season is designed for eight cases with four committed first (D27). The Night of
  Lanterns resolves the season's spine for real (the last Player is found, the festival returns, Pip belongs), and
  then leaves two or three warm threads open, so the child finishes satisfied and still wants more
  (`world-and-cases.md` §4.1). An open thread is an invitation, never an unresolved question of a solved case, and
  never a sales prompt (`engagement.md` §2).
- **How case 01 seeds it.** Kit's framed workshop photo (spec §2) shows her with four friends and four other puppets
  (a fox, a moth, a moon, a bell). The owl's crest on the booth matches a crest Pip will find again elsewhere.
  Paper bat sketch pages (spec §4) hint at the next places. All three are cheap additions to existing assets.
- **The recurring cast.** Growth arcs are the plan; each case design confirms its step.

| Character | Case 01 (spec) | Wants / worries | Grows across cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pip | the curious new kid, a girl (D30: she/her; feminine forms in Portuguese) | wants friends; worries she does not belong | from observer to the street's trusted detective |
| Bea | fast, sure, half right | wants to be taken seriously as the younger sister | learns to say "I saw… but I'm not sure"; becomes Pip's lookout on her bike |
| June | slipped in by the treehouse | wants adventure; keeps secrets | from rule-bender to partner; brings Pip into the Players' story |
| Mr. Vale | correct but limited view from his bench | worries he is only a watcher now | leaves his bench; his patient observation solves a later testimony |
| Aunt Kit | absent, then reopens the booth | misses the troupe | reveals the Players piece by piece; hosts the finale |

## 4. Mystery design rules (every case)

1. **Shape: question → explore → clue → connect → reveal.** Each case has 2–3 questions. A question appears when
   the world raises it, never as a task list.
2. **The true story comes first, and it is kind.** Write what actually happened, minute by minute, before any line.
   It must explain every clue and every testimony, contain no villain, and end with something restored or shared.
3. **Clues are physical and found by curiosity.** 3–5 per question family, each attracting attention diegetically
   (glint, flutter, sound, motion, smell made visible) and reachable on the mandatory route or with a demonstrated
   optional move. No UI arrow points at a clue.
4. **Testimonies are partial.** Each witness gives a true perception limited by position, attention or bias, and may
   be slightly wrong about meaning. At least one pair of testimonies seems to conflict and is reconciled by a place.
5. **The child makes the deduction** by placing clue stickers on a question (`mystery-first.md` §5). The game never
   states a conclusion before that.
6. **Hints never give the deduction.** They help with where to look and how to get there; tier 3 only demonstrates
   movement. A stuck deduction gets a musing and the missing clue's place, never the pairing.
7. **Every reveal pays off a clue the child found.** The answer comic is drawn from the child's stickers; the world
   reveal (a machine running, a door opening) is caused by the child's action.
8. **No deadlocks, early actions accepted** (spec §3 solvability): any order works, correct actions count early,
   lines stay temporally true, missing clues stay findable after closure.
9. **One honest decoy at most**, disprovable by touching or comparing, never by a stamp.
10. **Difficulty ramps by ideas, not by fiddliness:**

| Level | Cases | Slots | Decoys | Clue spread | Testimony | Moves needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01 | 2–3 | ≤ 1 | one block, 2 areas per question | one conflict | walk, jump |
| 2 | 02–04 | 3 | 1 | two places | one witness slightly wrong | plus one new move |
| 3 | 05–07 | 3, one question links two earlier answers | 1–2 | revisit an old place | a witness changes their story when shown a clue | combined moves |
| 4 | finale | answers of earlier cases become stickers | — | the whole neighbourhood | everyone remembers a piece | the child's choice |

## 5. Storytelling and UX rules (everywhere)

**Tone of voice.** Warm, short, concrete, a little funny. Sentences a 6-year-old can hear once and repeat (8 words
on average, 14 at most). Characters speak from their costume and habit (Bea fast, Vale slow and dry, June
conspiratorial, Kit theatrical). Spooky words are fine ("creak", "shadow", "knock"); fear words are not ("danger",
"monster", "dead"). Nobody is ever mocked for being wrong; witnesses are honest.

**Vignettes** (D23.1 hybrid: animation plus the words) may show what the speaker perceived, feels or wants, a place
to look, a sound, a gesture. They may **not** show an event the speaker did not witness, a conclusion, a route that
answers a question, a "correct" check, or a ring that marks a clue as important. Rules of `nonverbal-ux.md` §2
(one idea per beat, two moving parts, 2–4 s) stay.

**Captions** describe what is heard or seen, with direction, never what it means: "[Click!]" not "[The arm is back
in place.]".

**The notebook** shows questions, the child's found stickers and the child's solved answers. It may not show which
clue belongs to which question, the number of clues a question needs beyond its slots, or an answer before the
child's placement.

**New mechanics serve the mystery.** Every move or collectible must do at least one of: reach a clue or a vantage
point that changes what you understand; let you revisit an old place for something new; tell the street's story.
Paper bats are placed so following them teaches routes and vantage points, and their sketch pages carry lore and
seeds (`engagement.md`). A move that only adds challenge is optional and never on a clue's only path.

**Enterable places (D24, D24.1).** Interiors are closed, camera-ready rooms, seamless for small places and "bigger
on the inside" with a page-turn for large ones. Narratively, crossing a threshold is a chapter beat: the page-turn
is the notebook turning a page, so a new place feels like a new page of the mystery. Clues inside a room are visible
from the gameplay camera, never only from a cutaway.

**Portability (D25).** Every deduction and hint interaction works with touch, gamepad and keyboard (tap-tap placing,
cursor over the tray). No clue depends on haptics, colour alone, or sound alone (spec A11Y-01).

**Hybrid reading (D23.1).** Pictures carry meaning for pre-readers, words for readers and grown-ups reading along.
Both must say the same thing; neither may say more than the witness knows.

**Cutscenes** (owner, 2026-11-22: "if it makes sense to build cutscenes to help the narrative and story, that could
be good too"). A cutscene is a short, authored story moment the child watches. Rules:

- **Short and chunked.** About 10–40 s, in beats of a few seconds with one idea each (the vignette rules above),
  so a 6-year-old follows every beat.
- **Always skippable**, with any control scheme (D25), and **rewatchable** from the notebook (the case page). Each
  plays once per save on its own; the seen set lives outside the case save.
- **In-engine and original.** The real models, cameras and world, staged with character clips and gestures; no
  prerecorded video; all art by code (spec v1.3). Reduced motion cuts instead of blending.
- **Non-verbal with the hybrid text line** (D23.1): gesture, staging and captions carry meaning; babble voices and
  music arrive with audio (S003, D20).
- **Never ahead of the child.** A cutscene never reveals an answer before the child has deduced it, never names a
  conclusion, and never shows anything the child could discover by playing (a clue, a route, a hidden place). A
  world effect the child caused may be shown; the explanation stays in the notebook.
- **Used for:** (1) the opening: Pip arrives as the new kid on the street (boxes on the porch, a first look down
  the lane), ending on the arrival-porch composition so play starts there in control (spec §1, §3); (2) the case
  intro, where the world raises the question (a sound, a light, a strange motion), handing control back as soon as
  the question stands; (3) the reveal payoff after a right deduction, staged from the child's own stickers (the
  in-world twin of the answer comic); (4) "the street remembers" at the end of a case (the booth bows, Bea's owl,
  a new greeting); (5) the seed or gentle cliffhanger for the next case; (6) the season finale.
- **Not used for** tutorials, routes, clue reveals, hints or anything the child can do in play.

## 6. Design checklist (a stage plan or case passes all before it is built)

1. Is there a written true story, kind, villain-free, that explains every clue and testimony?
2. Does each question appear because the world raises it, not as a task?
3. Can every question be answered only by placing stickers the child found?
4. Does every clue have a diegetic attraction and a path without precision moves (or a demonstrated optional move)?
5. Is every testimony a perception, partial, from a stated position?
6. Does any vignette, caption, line, note or replay state a conclusion? (Must be no.)
7. Do hints point only to areas and demonstrate only moves?
8. Is there at most one decoy, disprovable by touch or comparison?
9. Does the reveal pay off clues the child found and an action the child took?
10. Does the case avoid deadlocks for every order of play (spec §3), with tests?
11. Does the street visibly remember the case afterwards (a changed place, a new greeting)?
12. Does the case plant a seed for a later case, and respect the continuity rules (`world-and-cases.md` §5)?
13. Does every new mechanic or collectible serve the mystery or exploration (§5)?
14. Do all strings live in `en.json` with context, keyed by stable IDs (CLAUDE.md)?
15. Does it fit the guardrails in `engagement.md` §2 (no timers, no FOMO, no purchases in the kids' experience)?
16. Is every cutscene short, skippable, rewatchable, in-engine, used only for the six purposes of §5, and free of
    any answer the child has not yet deduced or anything the child could discover by playing?

## 7. How the planned stages fit (recommended order)

| Order | Stage | Narrative role | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mystery-first core for case 01** (`mystery-first.md`) | makes the child the detective | every later stage builds on the deduction model; doing content first would be rework |
| 2 | **Audio S003** | knocks, the floor hum, the cloth's flutter, the wind wheel's whirr become clues and attractions; babble voices carry character | sound is the cheapest strong diegetic attraction and case 01's core clue is a rhythm |
| 3 | **More character actions** | testimony gestures (Bea shading her eyes up high, Vale tapping his watch), the hint glance toward an area, reactions to the child's discoveries | hints and testimonies now rely on bodies, not rings |
| 4 | **Paper bats with meaning** | exploration reward, route teacher, sketch pages that seed case 02 | needs the route and vantage points final; seeds need the arc approved |
| 5 | **Wall jump and ledge grab** | open revisit routes to case 01 extras and the first lore secrets | they need climbable geometry authored with clue placement in mind |
| 6 | **Dialogue portraits** | faces on stickers and bubbles that act (surprised, unsure) | polish: face discs already identify speakers |

**Cutscenes** follow the same order (`roadmap.md` §1): a small reusable system and case 01's opening and reveal with
the mystery-first foundations (S002.5b), their gesture and voice pass with character actions and audio (S003a),
the seed cutscene with the troupe photo (S004), and every other cutscene with its own case.

## 8. Owner answers (decided 2026-11-22, D27; questions kept for the record)

1. **Pip's backstory: new kid on the street?** (a) Yes, belonging is her arc. (b) She grew up here; the cases are
   about the street's past. **Decided (a)**, as recommended: it explains her curiosity and gives every case a
   friendship payoff.
2. **The big mystery: the Lantern Lane Players and the Night of Lanterns?** (a) Adopt it as the season's spine.
   (b) Keep cases standalone. **Decided (a):** the Players' machines wake one per case, leading to the Night of
   Lanterns, which resolves the spine and leaves later seasons an opening (§3).
3. **Cross-case memory.** (a) The street keeps visible changes from every solved case. (b) Each case resets the
   street. **Decided (a)**, within a small "street state" set (`world-and-cases.md` §5).
4. **Age band.** The spec said 8–12 with assisted play for younger; the owner's examples speak of 5–8. (a) Design
   the core for 6–9 and add optional "detective's extra" deductions for older children. (b) Keep 8–12. **Decided:
   6–9, with optional extras up to 12** (spec v1.4 amended).
5. **Cutscenes** (owner suggestion, 2026-11-22). **Decided:** yes, under the rules in §5 and checklist item 16.
